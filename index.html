Breakage-kernel analysis: SPIT plots the selected gravimetric breakage measure against ρdV² and fits one ordinary least-squares line through all valid repeat-level data. The fitted slope is reported as the breakability index; the x-intercept is the fitted zero-breakage transition point.

Sieve representative diameter: geometric mean = √(lower sieve × upper sieve).

Ghadiri–Zhang impact-attrition model

Reference: Ghadiri, M. & Zhang, Z. (2002), Impact attrition of particulate solids. Part 1: A theoretical model of chipping, Chemical Engineering Science, 57(17), 3659–3669. DOI: 10.1016/S0009-2509(02)00240-3.

The model addresses impact attrition of particulate solids exhibiting a semi-brittle failure mode. Material loss is associated with the formation and propagation of sub-surface lateral cracks, and the analysis is based on fracture mechanics of those cracks.

The underlying attrition-propensity description incorporates particle density, a characteristic linear particle dimension, impact velocity, hardness, fracture toughness (critical stress-intensity factor), and a constraint factor related to hardness and plastic yield stress. The model therefore links impact breakage with both impact conditions and material mechanical properties.

How SPIT uses the model: this analyser uses the experimental breakage-kernel representation with breakage extent plotted against ρdV². The fitted slope is reported as the breakability index and the fitted x-intercept as the zero-breakage transition point. This regression is an experimental characterisation used alongside the Ghadiri–Zhang model basis; it is not a replacement for the full fracture-mechanics material-property formulation.

Applicability: interpret the regression within the semi-brittle chipping/impact-attrition regime. The Ghadiri research group notes that impact-breakage models account for material mechanical properties and that a single-particle impact tester is one of the devices used for measuring particle breakage.
